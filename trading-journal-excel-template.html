Free trading journal template for Excel and Google Sheets

A spreadsheet is a perfectly good way to start a trading journal. Here is a template you can download, what each column is for, and the formulas that turn your trades into numbers worth reading.

Download the template

One file, 17 columns, three sample rows so you can see how it works. It is a plain CSV, so it opens in Excel, Google Sheets, LibreOffice and Numbers.

Download the CSV template

In Google Sheets: File, Import, Upload, then choose "Replace spreadsheet". The three example rows have SAMPLE in the symbol column. Delete them before you start.

The columns, and why each one is there

The aim is to record only what you will actually use later. Seventeen columns sounds like a lot, but most of them you will fill in about ten seconds after a trade, and the rest you can add during the review.

The formulas to add

Trades start in row 2. In the template the columns are: F Setup, G Entry, H Stop-loss, I Target, J Exit, K Quantity, L Planned risk, M Charges, N Net P&L, O R multiple. If you move columns, change the letters to match.

  • Net P&L, for a buy: =(J2 - G2) * K2 - M2. For a sell, use =(G2 - J2) * K2 - M2.
  • R multiple: =N2 / L2
  • Win rate: =COUNTIF(N2:N500, ">0") / COUNT(N2:N500)
  • Average win: =AVERAGEIF(N2:N500, ">0"), and for the average loss use "<0"
  • Expectancy per trade, in R: =AVERAGE(O2:O500). If this is above zero after charges, the strategy has made money on average.
  • Results by setup: =SUMIF(F2:F500, "Breakout", N2:N500), one line for each setup name.

To see how your win rate and average win compare to what you need, put your numbers into the break-even calculator.

What to review, and when

  • Every day, two minutes. Fill in any blanks. Tag each mistake while you still remember it.
  • Every week, twenty minutes. Sort by setup and by mistake tag. Which setup paid? Which mistake cost the most? Pick one thing to change next week, and only one.
  • Every month. Check expectancy and the longest losing streak against what you expected. If you are not sure what to expect, this table shows how long a normal bad run lasts.

Do not draw conclusions from fewer than about 50 trades. A single month is mostly luck.

Common questions

What should a trading journal include?

At minimum: the date, what you traded, your entry, stop and exit, the quantity, the net result, and why you took the trade. The reason is the part people skip, and it is the part that lets you learn from it.

Excel or Google Sheets?

Either. Google Sheets is easier to open on a phone and to share with a mentor. Excel is faster with a lot of rows. The template works in both.

Should I record paper trades?

Yes, if you mark them as paper. They count toward the sample you need before you trust any number, but do not mix them with real trades when you work out your win rate.
