Common trading mistakes and how a journal catches them

Most losing months have a pattern. Here are seven common mistakes and a simple way to measure what each one costs.

Why track mistakes at all

Most traders blame the market for a bad month. A journal often shows something different: the same few habits repeated. Tagging mistakes turns a vague feeling ("I trade badly sometimes") into a number you can see, such as "my revenge trades lost ₹14,000 this quarter".

Seven mistakes worth tagging

  • FOMO. Entering because the price is running and you are afraid to miss it, not because your setup appeared.
  • Moved stop-loss. Pushing the stop further away after the trade went against you.
  • Oversized. Taking a bigger quantity than your risk rule allows.
  • No plan. Trading without a written entry, stop and target before you click.
  • Chased entry. Buying well past your planned price, so the stop is now far away and the reward small.
  • Revenge trade. A trade taken straight after a loss, to win the money back.
  • Early exit. Closing a good trade before your target out of nervousness.

These are the mistake tags built into the TradeMath journal. You tick them when you log or edit a trade.

How to measure what a mistake costs

  1. Tag each trade honestly, including the ones that happened to win.
  2. After a few weeks, filter the trades by tag.
  3. Add up the profit and loss of the tagged trades and compare it with the trades that had no mistake.

If your untagged trades make money and your tagged ones lose it, the problem is discipline, not the method. That is a much more useful finding than "the market was hard".

A simple weekly review

  • Read the week's trades in order, not only the biggest ones.
  • Tag the mistakes, then pick the one that cost the most. Work on that one rule for the following week.
  • Check whether your actual win rate and average win and loss match what you assumed. See expectancy.

Common questions

What is revenge trading?

Revenge trading is entering a trade right after a loss to win the money back quickly. It is driven by emotion rather than a setup, and the position is often larger than normal, which can turn one loss into several.

How do I stop overtrading?

Set a maximum number of trades per day before the session starts, and stop when you reach it. TradeMath can show a bar that tracks your trades against that limit.

Should I tag winning trades that had a mistake?

Yes. A trade can win despite a mistake. Tagging it keeps the habit visible, which is the point, because the same habit may cost you next time.

How long before a journal shows patterns?

A few weeks of honest entries is usually enough for repeated mistakes to stand out. More trades make the numbers more reliable.
