Drawdown and recovery explained: why a 50% loss needs a 100% gain

A loss is easy to make and harder to win back. Here is what drawdown means, the recovery table, and how a small risk per trade keeps it in check.

What drawdown means

Drawdown is the fall from your account's highest value to its lowest value afterwards. If your account reaches ₹1,20,000 and then drops to ₹96,000, the drawdown is ₹24,000, which is 20% of the peak.

Drawdown % = (Peak − Current value) ÷ Peak × 100

The maximum drawdown is the largest such fall over a period. It shows the worst pain a method would have put you through, which the average return hides.

Why recovery is harder than the loss

A percentage loss and the percentage gain needed to undo it are not the same, because the gain is measured on a smaller balance.

Gain needed = 1 ÷ (1 − Loss %) − 1

Losing 50% needs a 100% gain just to get back to where you started. This is why many traders put so much weight on avoiding deep drawdowns, and why a small risk per trade matters.

A worked example

You start with ₹1,00,000 and the account falls to ₹80,000, a 20% drawdown. To recover, ₹80,000 must grow by ₹20,000, which is 25% on the current balance. If it falls to ₹60,000 instead (40%), recovery needs ₹40,000, or 66.7%.

How to keep drawdowns smaller

  • Risk a small, fixed amount per trade. Smaller losses mean a slower slide during a bad run.
  • Count your open risk. Several trades at once add up.
  • Set a daily loss limit. TradingMath lets you set a maximum daily loss and shows a bar that warns you as you near it.
  • Review a streak. After a run of losses, check the journal for repeated mistakes before you size up again.

Common questions

What is a good maximum drawdown?

There is no universal figure. It depends on your method and how much loss you can live with without breaking your rules. What matters is that you know your own number and that your risk per trade keeps you inside it.

Why does a 50% loss need a 100% gain?

Because the gain is measured on what is left. Losing half of ₹1,00,000 leaves ₹50,000, and doubling ₹50,000 is the only way to get back to ₹1,00,000.

How is drawdown different from a single losing trade?

A losing trade is one result. Drawdown is the cumulative fall from the account's peak across many trades, so a series of small losses builds into a large drawdown.

How do I track drawdown?

Keep a record of your account value after each trade or day, and note the highest value so far. The journal's equity curve and monthly reports make the falls easy to see.
