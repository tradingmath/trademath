MIS vs CNC (intraday vs delivery): margin, leverage and risk

MIS and CNC are the two products most Indian stock traders choose between. Here is what each means and why leverage changes the cash you need, not the loss you can take.

The product types in plain words

  • CNC (delivery). You pay the full value, the shares are credited to your demat account, and you can hold them as long as you like.
  • MIS (intraday). You trade on margin and must close the position the same day. If you do not, the broker closes it for you before the market ends, often with an extra charge. The auto square-off time differs between brokers.
  • NRML. The normal product for futures and options if you want to carry the position overnight, with the full margin blocked.

Names, leverage and rules vary by broker, so treat these as the general picture and check your own broker's terms.

Leverage reduces cash, not risk

With MIS a broker may let you trade a position worth several times the cash you put up. That is leverage. It changes how much money is blocked. It does not change how much you can lose, which is still the quantity times the distance to your stop-loss.

Example: entry ₹500, stop-loss ₹495, risk ₹1,000. The quantity is 1,000 ÷ 5 = 200 shares, a trade value of ₹1,00,000. The loss if the stop is hit is ₹1,000 either way.

  • CNC: about ₹1,00,000 of cash is needed for the 200 shares.
  • MIS at an example leverage of 5×: the broker may block about ₹20,000 as margin.

The risk is the same ₹1,000. What changes is that leverage lets you open a bigger position on less cash, which makes it easier to take a size that is too large for your account. That is why it helps to size from your risk first and only then check the margin.

Why the quantity does not change with leverage

TradingMath calculates the quantity from your risk and stop-loss, then shows the margin that quantity needs under the leverage you pick. If the margin is more than your capital, it warns you and shows the most you could afford.

Other differences to know

  • Charges. Brokerage and taxes differ between intraday and delivery trades. The rates change, so check your broker's current charges.
  • Holding. An intraday trade cannot be carried to the next day, so a stop-loss and exit plan must fit inside the session.
  • Gap risk. A delivery position is exposed to overnight news. An intraday position is not, but it can still move fast during the day.

Common questions

Does MIS leverage increase my risk?

Not by itself. Your loss is set by quantity and stop-loss distance. But leverage lets you open a larger position on less cash, so it is easier to take more risk than intended. Size from your risk first.

What happens if I do not square off an MIS trade?

The broker usually closes it automatically before the market ends, and may charge a fee. The cut-off time and fee differ between brokers, so check yours.

What is the difference between CNC and MIS?

CNC is delivery: you pay in full and can hold the shares. MIS is intraday margin trading: you put up part of the value, and the position must be closed the same day.

How much margin will MIS need?

It depends on the stock, the broker and the day, and it can change. TradingMath lets you pick a leverage to estimate it, but your broker's margin calculator has the real number.
