How much should you risk per trade? The 1% rule explained

Risk per trade is the amount you are willing to lose if your stop-loss is hit. Here is how to think about it, with rupee examples and the effect of losing streaks.

What risk per trade means

Risk per trade is the amount you will lose if your stop-loss is hit. Most traders write it as a percentage of their capital, because a percentage stays meaningful as the account grows or shrinks.

Risk in rupees = Capital × Risk %

That rupee figure then decides your size. Divide it by the loss per share (entry minus stop-loss) and you have the quantity. The position size guide shows the full formula.

The 1% rule in rupees

The 1% rule says to risk no more than about 1% of your capital on any one trade. It is a rule of thumb, not a law. Here is what different percentages mean on a ₹1,00,000 account:

Risk per tradeRupees at ₹1,00,000 capital
0.5%₹500
1%₹1,000
2%₹2,000
5%₹5,000

What a losing streak does

Every trader has losing streaks, even with a good method. The table shows the balance after a run of consecutive losses, starting from ₹1,00,000 and losing the same percentage each time:

Losing streakAt 1% riskAt 2% riskAt 5% risk
5 losses in a row₹95,099 (-4.9%)₹90,392 (-9.6%)₹77,378 (-22.6%)
10 losses in a row₹90,438 (-9.6%)₹81,707 (-18.3%)₹59,874 (-40.1%)
20 losses in a row₹81,791 (-18.2%)₹66,761 (-33.2%)₹35,849 (-64.2%)

At 1% risk, ten losses in a row cost under 10% of the account. At 5% risk, the same streak takes away about 40%, and getting that back needs a much bigger gain (see drawdown and recovery). A small risk per trade is what lets you survive the bad runs.

How traders choose their number

  • Win rate and reward. A method that wins less often has longer losing streaks, which argues for a smaller risk per trade.
  • Experience. Many traders start with a very small percentage while they learn how their own method behaves.
  • Open positions. If you hold several trades at once, the risks add up. Five trades at 1% each is closer to 5% at risk together.
  • Your comfort. The best number is one you can follow without panicking after a loss.

There is no figure that suits everyone, and this guide does not tell you what to pick.

Percentage risk or a fixed rupee amount

  • Percentage of current balance: risk grows after wins and shrinks after losses. It slows down a drawdown automatically.
  • Fixed rupee amount: simple and steady, for example ₹1,000 on every trade. It does not adjust as the balance changes, so review it now and then.

The TradeMath calculator supports both: type a percentage, or switch to a flat rupee amount.

Common questions

Is 1% risk per trade too small?

It feels small, but it keeps a long losing streak survivable. On ₹1,00,000, 1% is ₹1,000 a trade. Many traders find that boring sizing is what keeps them in the game long enough to learn.

Can I risk 2% or 5% per trade?

You can, but the damage from a bad run grows quickly. Ten losses in a row at 2% take about 18% off the account, and at 5% about 40%. Higher risk also means bigger swings in both directions.

Does leverage change how much I risk?

No. Your risk is set by your stop-loss distance and your quantity. Leverage only changes how much cash the broker blocks as margin. See the MIS vs CNC guide.

Should I change my risk percentage after a loss?

Some traders keep it fixed so that one bad day does not change their rules. Others lower it after a drawdown. Decide the rule in advance, not in the middle of a losing run.
