The formula

Break-even win rate = 1 ÷ (1 + reward ratio)

If you risk ₹1,000 to make ₹2,000, the ratio is 1:2 and the break-even win rate is 1 ÷ 3, about 33.3%. Win more often than that and, before costs, you are making money. Win less often and you are losing it, however good each winning trade feels.

With costs, the number rises. Brokerage, taxes, slippage and the spread are paid on every trade, winners and losers alike:

Break-even win rate = (risk + cost) ÷ (risk + reward)

Take ₹1,000 risk, ₹2,000 reward and ₹50 of costs per trade. That gives (1,000 + 50) ÷ 3,000 = 35.0%. A strategy that wins 38% of the time then earns 0.38 × 2,000 − 0.62 × 1,000 − 50 = ₹90 per trade. Small, but positive. Drop to 34% and the same strategy loses about ₹30 a trade.

The cost column matters most at low ratios. At 1:1 a ₹50 cost adds 2.5 points to the win rate you need. That is a lot when the edge you are chasing is only a few points wide.

Why a bigger ratio is not automatically better

The table makes 1:5 look easy: you only need to win one trade in six. The catch is that the target is five times as far away as the stop, so price reaches it less often. Win rate and ratio are not independent dials. Push the target out and your win rate usually falls, sometimes by more than the table can forgive.

The calculator cannot tell you your real win rate. Only your own trade history can. Log your trades for a few months and use your actual numbers, not the ones you hope for. If you do not have a record yet, our free journal template is a place to start.

A few things people get wrong

  • Using the planned ratio, not the realised one. If you plan 1:2 but cut winners early and let losers run a bit, your real ratio is nearer 1:1.2. Work it out from your closed trades.
  • Ignoring costs on small targets. On a ₹5 stop, a few rupees of charges per share is a big slice of the risk.
  • Treating break-even as the goal. Break-even only means no profit and no loss. You want a margin above it, because the estimate of your win rate carries error, especially in the first hundred trades.
  • Looking at one number. Ratio, win rate and costs work together. A 70% win rate at 1:0.3 loses money. A 30% win rate at 1:3 can make it.

Common questions

What is a good risk-reward ratio?

There is no universal answer. It depends on how often you win. Many traders aim for at least 1:2 so they can be wrong more often than they are right, but a scalper with a 70% win rate can do well at lower ratios.

Is a 1:2 ratio enough to be profitable?

It is enough if you win more than about 33% of the time after costs. Below that it is not, whatever the ratio.

How do I find my actual ratio?

Divide your average winning trade by your average losing trade, from your closed trades. If your average win is ₹1,400 and your average loss is ₹1,000, your ratio is 1:1.4.
