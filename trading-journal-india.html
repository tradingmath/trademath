Trading journal for Indian traders: what to track and how to review

A journal turns memory into data. Here is what to record, which numbers to watch, and a simple review routine.

Why keep a trading journal

Most traders remember their best trades and forget the rest. A journal replaces memory with facts. After a few weeks it shows your real win rate, average win, average loss and which setups actually pay, so you can stop guessing and fix specific habits.

What to record for every trade

  • Date, instrument and direction (long or short, stock, futures, option).
  • Entry, stop-loss, target and quantity. The plan, written before the trade.
  • Exit price and charges, so the profit shown is the net figure.
  • The setup: why you took it, such as breakout, pullback or news.
  • Mistakes: moved the stop, entered early, oversized, ignored the plan.
  • Notes: how you felt and what you would do differently.

The numbers that matter

MeasureWhat it tells you
Win rateHow often you are right
Average win and average lossWhether winners are bigger than losers
Realised risk-rewardWhat you actually achieved, not what you planned
Expectancy per tradeWin % × average win − loss % × average loss. See risk-reward explained
Results by setupWhich strategies to keep and which to drop
Results by mistake tagWhich habit costs you the most money

How to review

  1. Weekly: read the week's trades. Tag mistakes honestly.
  2. Monthly: look at expectancy by setup and drop the weakest. Check whether your stops and sizes followed the plan.
  3. After a losing streak: check whether you broke your rules or whether the loss was within normal variation.

What should a trading journal include?

Date, instrument, direction, entry, stop-loss, target, quantity, exit, charges, the setup, mistakes and notes. The plan should be written before the trade.

How often should I review my journal?

Read your trades weekly and review statistics monthly, especially expectancy by setup and the cost of your repeated mistakes.
