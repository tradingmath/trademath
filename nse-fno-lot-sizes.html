NSE F&O lot sizes: indices and stocks

Lot sizes for index and stock futures and options, how they affect your risk, and a reminder to confirm the latest figure with the exchange.

Check before you trade. NSE revises lot sizes, and contracts can be added or removed. The stock lot sizes below match the exchange list as of 30 October 2026. Confirm the current lot size with the NSE circular or your broker's contract list.

What a lot size is

Futures and options trade in fixed bundles called lots. A lot size of 500 means one contract controls 500 shares. Your profit or loss is the price move multiplied by the lot size, and the margin and premium you pay scale with it too.

How to use the lot size

  • Risk per lot = loss per unit at your stop × lot size. Compare it with your risk budget. See how to calculate position size.
  • Value of one lot = price × lot size. For an option buyer, the premium × lot size is the most you can lose on that lot. For a seller, the margin required is much larger and the loss is not capped at the premium.
  • Margin depends on the contract, volatility and your broker. Check your broker's margin calculator for the real figure.

The TradeMath calculator fills in the lot size when you pick a symbol and shows the risk per lot.

Common questions

What is the lot size of Nifty?

The Nifty lot size in the exchange list used for this page is 65. Lot sizes are revised by NSE, so confirm the current figure with NSE or your broker.

Can lot sizes change?

Yes. The exchange revises lot sizes from time to time, and it can add or remove stocks from the F&O list. Always check the latest NSE circular.

How do I find the margin for one lot?

Use your broker's margin calculator. Margin depends on the contract, volatility and whether you buy or sell, so a fixed number on a web page can be out of date.
