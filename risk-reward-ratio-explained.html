Risk-reward ratio explained: win rate, expectancy and break-even

A good ratio lets you be wrong often and still make money. Here is the maths, a break-even table and how to set targets.

What risk-reward means

The risk-reward ratio compares what you stand to lose with what you aim to gain. If your stop-loss is ₹5 below your entry and your target is ₹10 above it, the trade is 1 : 2: you risk 1 to make 2.

Risk-reward = (Target − Entry) ÷ (Entry − Stop-loss)

The win rate you need to break even

A better ratio means you can be wrong more often and still not lose money. Ignoring charges, the minimum win rate to break even is:

Break-even win rate = 1 ÷ (1 + reward-to-risk)

1 : 0.566.7%You must win two of every three trades
1 : 150.0%Win half your trades
1 : 1.540.0%Win four of every ten
1 : 233.3%Win one in three
1 : 325.0%Win one in four

Real results are worse than this table, because brokerage, STT, exchange charges and GST are paid on every trade, wins and losses alike. Add them to your loss and subtract them from your gain before you judge a ratio.

Expectancy: the number that matters

Ratio alone does not tell you whether a method works. Expectancy combines win rate and size:

Expectancy per trade = (Win % × average win) − (Loss % × average loss)

Example: you win 40% of trades with an average win of ₹2,000, and lose 60% with an average loss of ₹1,000. Expectancy = 0.40 × 2,000 − 0.60 × 1,000 = ₹800 − ₹600 = ₹200 per trade, before charges. You can be right less than half the time and still be profitable, if the winners are larger than the losers.

That is why a trading journal matters. Your actual win rate and average win and loss tell you your real expectancy. A guess does not.

How to set a target

  • Start from the stop-loss, which should sit where your trade idea is proven wrong, then place the target at a multiple of that distance, such as 1.5× or 2×.
  • Check that the target is reachable. A 1 : 3 target that sits beyond a strong resistance level may rarely be hit.
  • Use the same ratio rule every time, so your results can be compared.

Common mistakes

  • Chasing a high ratio. A 1 : 5 target that is almost never reached gives a low win rate and a worse expectancy than 1 : 2.
  • Forgetting charges on tight trades, where they can eat a large part of a small target.
  • Moving targets and stops during the trade, which breaks the ratio you planned.

Common questions

What is a good risk-reward ratio?

Many traders look for at least 1 : 1.5 or 1 : 2, but the right ratio depends on your win rate. Expectancy, which combines the win rate with the size of wins and losses, is what shows whether a method works.

How do I calculate the risk-reward ratio?

Divide the distance from entry to target by the distance from entry to stop-loss. A ₹5 stop with a ₹10 target is 1 : 2.

What win rate do I need at 1 : 2?

About 33.3% before charges. Brokerage and taxes raise the number you need slightly.
