How much should you risk per trade? The 1% rule explained

Risk per trade is the most you are willing to lose if your stop-loss is hit. Keeping it small is what lets an account survive the losing streaks every strategy has.

What risk per trade means

Risk per trade is the dollar amount you lose if the trade goes against you and your stop is hit. It is usually written as a percentage of your account. On a $20,000 account, risking 1% means a stopped-out trade costs about $200.

Risk is not the same as position size. You can risk 1% of your account in a position that is worth 25% of it, if your stop is close. The position size formula turns a risk amount into a number of shares.

The 1% rule

The "1% rule" says to risk no more than about 1% of your account on any single trade. It is a rule of thumb, not a law or a guarantee. Some traders choose 0.5%, some 2%. What matters is that the number is small, decided in advance, and kept fixed.

Why small risk matters: what a losing streak costs

Losses compound. The table shows what ten losing trades in a row would do to an account at different risk levels, and the gain you would then need to get back to where you started.

1%90.4%9.6%10.6%
2%81.7%18.3%22.4%
5%59.9%40.1%67.0%
10%34.9%65.1%186.8%

Ten losses in a row is not rare for a strategy with a win rate below 50%. At 1% risk you are down about 10% and can recover with a modest gain. At 10% risk you have lost almost two thirds of the account and need to nearly triple what is left. These figures are simple arithmetic, not a forecast.

How to choose your number

  • Start smaller than you think. You can raise risk later if you have a record that justifies it. It is much harder to recover from a large early loss.
  • Match it to your strategy. A strategy that wins 35% of the time needs smaller risk than one that wins 65%, because it will have longer losing streaks.
  • Think in dollars too. If losing $500 in one trade would make you act badly on the next, your risk is too big for your account, whatever the percentage says.
  • Set a daily loss limit. Many traders stop for the day after losing a fixed amount, such as two or three times their per-trade risk.

Risk is not reward

Risk per trade only sets how much a loss costs. Whether a strategy makes money also depends on how often it wins and how large the wins are compared with the losses. Test your own numbers in a journal rather than assuming a strategy works.

Common questions

What percentage of my account should I risk per trade?

Many traders keep it between 0.5% and 2%. A smaller number survives losing streaks better. There is no number that guarantees profit.

Does risking 1% mean I only invest 1% of my account?

No. It means a stopped-out trade loses about 1%. The position can be much bigger than 1% of the account when your stop is close to your entry.

Should I risk the same amount on every trade?

Most risk-focused traders do, as a percentage of the current account. Raising risk after a win, or doubling after a loss, makes results swing more and is a common way accounts are damaged.
