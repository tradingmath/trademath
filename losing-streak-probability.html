How many losing trades in a row should you expect?

Most new traders plan for a bad run of three or four trades. If your win rate is 40%, the odds say you will probably see nine or more in a row within a year. This page shows the numbers, and lets you try your own.

The mistake behind a lot of blown accounts

A trader with a 40% win rate hears "you lose 60% of the time" and pictures something like win, lose, lose, win, lose. Losses spread out evenly, never more than two or three together. That is not how chance behaves. Each trade is a separate draw, so losses clump, and the clumps are longer than almost anyone expects.

This matters because the size of your position has to survive the clump, not the average. If you size for three losses in a row and get nine, you are in a hole before the strategy has had a chance to work.

The table

For each win rate and number of trades, the first figure is the median longest losing streak: half of all traders running this strategy would see a longer streak, and half a shorter one. The figure in brackets is the chance of 8 or more losses in a row at some point.

Read one row to see how it plays out. At a 40% win rate, 250 trades is roughly one year of three trades a day on 80 trading days, or one trade a day for a full year. The median longest streak is 9 losses, and there is an 83% chance of seeing at least 8 in a row. Even at 60%, you should expect a run of about 5 over 250 trades.

How these numbers were worked out

We did not copy a table from somewhere. For each cell, the chance of a losing run of a given length was calculated exactly by stepping through the trades one at a time and tracking the current run of losses. We then checked three cells against a simulation of 60,000 runs each. The averages matched to within 0.02 of a trade, and the 8-in-a-row odds matched to the third decimal place in two of the three cases and within 0.002 in the other.

The assumption to be aware of is independence. We treat each trade as a separate draw with a fixed win rate. Real strategies drift in and out of favour, so a bad month often really is a bad month, and streaks in live trading tend to be longer than these. Take the table as a floor.

What a streak costs at different risk levels

Say your plan is 40% wins over 250 trades, so you should expect to meet about 9 to 12 losses in a row at some point. Here is what 12 straight losses does to the account:

Risk per tradeLoss after 12 in a rowGain needed to recover
1%11.4%12.8%
2%21.5%27.4%
5%46.0%85.1%

At 1% the streak is a bad few weeks. At 5% it is close to the end of the account, and you would need to nearly double what is left. Same strategy, same luck, very different outcome, and the only thing that changed is the position size. The full reasoning is in our guide on how much to risk per trade.

What to do with this

  • Look up your own row before you trade. Find your win rate and the number of trades you expect in a year, and write down the streak you should plan for.
  • Decide the most the streak is allowed to cost you. Many traders pick somewhere between 10% and 20% of the account. Work backwards to a risk per trade that keeps a typical streak inside it.
  • Do not read a streak as proof that the strategy broke. A run of eight losses at a 40% win rate is ordinary. Judge a strategy over a few hundred trades, not twenty.
  • Test it before it costs money. The TradingMath Planner simulates a strategy trade by trade, so you can see how the account moves through a streak.

Common questions

Does a high win rate protect me from long streaks?

It shortens them, but does not remove them. At a 60% win rate the median longest streak over 250 trades is 5, and there is still about a 9% chance of seeing 8 or more.

How many trades should I look at before judging a strategy?

There is no magic number, but fewer than 100 tells you very little. At 40% wins, a perfectly good strategy can show 7 or more straight losses inside its first 100 trades about half the time.

Are losing streaks more likely after a loss?

In the model, no: each trade is independent. In real life, your behaviour changes after losses, and so do markets. Both can make streaks longer, which is why a daily loss limit helps.
